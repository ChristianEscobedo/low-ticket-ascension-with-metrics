Understanding The Knowledge Gap | Curiosity Loop
Lowenstein's GAP Theory - in a recent study, a group of subjects were tested on what would create the most curiosity.
The subjects were tested in a game of trivia to see how they would engage. The study found that the participants were the least interested in the trivia when they either already knew the answer or were already extremely confident… But, when they came across a question that they had ‘some idea’ about the answer yet lacked confidence or ‘gaps’, their curiosity skyrocketed. 
Their compulsion to know the answer was so great that they were even willing to pay for the information even though it was revealed for free after the session...
The knowledge gap is a highly effective way to create curiosity. It will reverse the conversation when someone is trying to sell you, and create an environment where they are wanting you to sell them. Just like in Lowenstein's GAP Theory experiment. Credit - I learned this from Adam Lucerne of PDOCS 
Here's a method on how I use the Knowledge Gap in DM's
Scenario: Let's say someone has reached out to me in the DM's clearly trying to sell me on their program.
They send a DM saying...
"Hello, Christian!  How are you? Lovely to meet you!  I am looking to grow my network. What do you do?"
I Reply
Hello [Name] I'm doing great thanks. Would love to be a part of your network (I'm genuinely curious and giving them what they want) I am a coach. How about you? 
They Reply.
Awesome, I love that, I help X do X (they might reply with another Question, if so just answer and reply with a knowledge gap.
I reply with a knowledge gap...
We do a similar thing, however, we [Insert Knowledge gap].’ Are you [Question about knowledge gap]? -  SEND
Example: We do a similar thing, however, we helped our clients turn a book they have written (product) into a program, now they can charge $3k-$10k instead of $15-$30 dollars and create a much bigger impact with the same exact audience. Have you ever considered offering a High Ticket Program?
They Reply
In most cases the potential prospect will ask how you do it or want to know more about the knowledge gap.
This is incredibly powerful and reverses the frame so that you are in a great position to provide value and create a deep lasting connection with someone who was originally trying to sell you.
What's the best way to make someone curious about your next step? It starts with something they already know, and then you create an unexpected solution that creates curiosity. All of this is done by incorporating "knowledge gap/curiosity loops" in a conversation.



Before we dive into creating an offer, let’s geek out on some psychology. We are going to reverse engineer a thing called ‘cognitive dissonance’ by using a Cognitive Dissonance Map. 

Ever heard of the term ‘cognitive dissonance’? This is a term used to describe the ‘metal discomfort of holding conflicting beliefs, behavior or attitudes. 

Let’s say for example, that you are a fitness coach...but you also smoke a pack of cigarettes a day. 

Your belief = Health is important and ‘my body is a temple’ 

Your behavior = Smoking cigarettes which is literally killing your body 

The result? You feel uneasy. You feel physical mental discomfort. Something has to change...

This is cognitive dissonance and it’s one of the most powerful tools in your arsenal, as when you speak with prospects, they will have a certain belief like ‘the way to getting more income is just working harder’ but then when you come in with a new belief like ‘the way to earn more income is not by working harder but by working smarter’ than your prospect will have this conflicting discomfort. 

So what happens after you have introduced cognitive dissonance? 

Once you create cognitive dissonance by introducing a new belief or behavior that conflicts  there are 3 things that people will do to ease the pain. 

Change the belief with Outside Sources- They will look for outside sources to prove you are wrong and change the belief entirely. They will ask for case studies, ask to speak to other clients or even go on a google rampage to find out more about you as they are trying to find something that supports their own beliefs. 

Example: The fitness instructor who is a smoker might go look at sources to try to find one article explaining how it’s not that bad. Or ask friends what they think to try to find confirmation of their negative behavior.  

They will change the attitude about the behavior/belief - This means they will ‘downplay it’ or change the attitude entirely. 

This actually happens in 2 ways: 

They change the importance of the belief and downplay it with a new attitude. 
They change the importance of the belief with self doubt convincing themselves it won’t work for them. 

Example: A fitness instructor who smokes, may justify this by saying they know people who are doing far worse and the fact they eat so healthy, they can have one bad habit. 

Or they may justify it by saying ‘quitting is too hard’ and not have any clear steps so nothing changes. 

They will change their behavior - they will take action. They will accept the new belief and adjust their attitude and behavior.  This is what we want! 

Example: Fitness instructor who smokes, accepting it does not align with their beliefs and quitting. 

So What’s the Take Away? You have to create cognitive dissonance by introducing a new idea THEN make it so the only way forward is to change the behavior.

There are 7 elements you need: 

Attention (the surface level problems that get someone's attention) 
Acknowledgement (Acknowledging all their problems in their own messaging)
Agitate (Introducing the claim of the real problem you are solving thus creating cognitive dissonance.) 
Authority (How you can deliver, how you can prove you delivered, the degree of certainty, sophistication and time - Otherwise they will seek outside sources) 
Angst (The cost of doing nothing and not fixing this problem - otherwise it won’t be important enough to them)
Ambiguity (The exact steps you would need to break down to solve this problem - otherwise they will doubt it will work for them) 
Appeal (What is the cost, and are there any additional services you can add on to make the decision a no-brainer - Create immediate action)
